# Chapter 02 — DCF and Trading Comps from First Principles

This chapter is a complete corporate finance walkthrough of how Northstar Components is valued from first principles.

---

## 1. The DCF Building Blocks

A Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) model estimates the intrinsic value of a company based on the present value of its future cash flows.

```text
Historical Financials (2023A–2025A)
                 ↓
      Revenue & Margin Forecast (2026E–2030E)
                 ↓
     Unlevered Free Cash Flow (UFCF)
                 ↓
       Discount Rate (WACC via CAPM)
                 ↓
Terminal Value (Gordon Growth) + Present Value
                 ↓
       DCF Enterprise Value (EV)
                 ↓
Less: Net Debt (Gross Debt − Cash)
                 ↓
      Implied Equity Value & Share Price
```

Let's walk through every single formula using the actual Northstar numbers.

---

## 2. Step 1: Revenue & Margin Projections (2026E–2030E)

### Revenue Projection
Management guided to "high single digits" for FY2026. The gold model interprets this as **8.0% growth** from 2025A (\$1,000.0mm):

$$\text{Revenue}_{2026E} = \$1,000.0 \times (1 + 0.08) = \$1,080.0\text{mm}$$

Over the 5-year forecast horizon, growth gently tapers to a long-term steady-state rate:

| Forecast Year | 2026E | 2027E | 2028E | 2029E | 2030E |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Growth Rate** | 8.0% | 7.0% | 6.0% | 5.0% | 4.0% |
| **Revenue (\$mm)** | **1,080.00** | **1,155.60** | **1,224.94** | **1,286.18** | **1,337.63** |

---

## 3. Step 2: From EBITDA to NOPAT (Operating Profit After Tax)

For each projection year, we compute the operational income waterfall:

### EBITDA & Margin
Management targets approximately 17% margin in 2026E, expanding toward 18.5% in 2030E:
$$\text{EBITDA}_{2026E} = \$1,080.00 \times 17.0\% = \$183.60\text{mm}$$

### Depreciation & Amortization (D&A)
Historically stable at **4.0% of revenue**:
$$\text{D&A}_{2026E} = \$1,080.00 \times 4.0\% = \$43.20\text{mm}$$

### Operating Income (GAAP EBIT)
$$\text{EBIT}_{2026E} = \text{EBITDA} - \text{D&A} = \$183.60 - \$43.20 = \$140.40\text{mm}$$

### Net Operating Profit After Tax (NOPAT)
Assuming a statutory tax rate $t = 25.0\%$:
$$\text{NOPAT}_{2026E} = \text{EBIT} \times (1 - t) = \$140.40 \times (1 - 0.25) = \$105.30\text{mm}$$

---

## 4. Step 3: Deriving Unlevered Free Cash Flow (UFCF)

Unlevered Free Cash Flow represents cash generated by core operations available to **all capital providers** (both debt and equity holders) after reinvestment in working capital and fixed assets:

$$\text{UFCF} = \text{NOPAT} + \text{D&A} - \text{Capex} - \Delta\text{NWC}$$

Let's compute each reinvestment component for 2026E:

1. **Capital Expenditures (Capex)**: Historically 4.5% of sales:
   $$\text{Capex}_{2026E} = \$1,080.00 \times 4.5\% = \$48.60\text{mm}$$
2. **Net Working Capital (NWC)**: Historically 12.0% of sales:
   $$\text{NWC}_{2026E} = \$1,080.00 \times 12.0\% = \$129.60\text{mm}$$
   From 2025A NWC of \$120.00mm, the change in working capital is:
   $$\Delta\text{NWC}_{2026E} = \$129.60 - \$120.00 = +\$9.60\text{mm} \quad (\text{cash outflow})$$

3. **UFCF Calculation (2026E)**:
   $$\text{UFCF}_{2026E} = \$105.30 + \$43.20 - \$48.60 - \$9.60 = \mathbf{\$90.30\text{mm}}$$

### 5-Year UFCF Schedule

| Metric (\$mm) | 2026E | 2027E | 2028E | 2029E | 2030E |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Revenue** | 1,080.00 | 1,155.60 | 1,224.94 | 1,286.18 | 1,337.63 |
| **EBIT** | 140.40 | 156.01 | 171.49 | 183.28 | 193.96 |
| **NOPAT (25% tax)** | 105.30 | 117.00 | 128.62 | 137.46 | 145.47 |
| **(+) D&A (4.0%)** | 43.20 | 46.22 | 49.00 | 51.45 | 53.51 |
| **(−) Capex (4.5%)** | (48.60) | (52.00) | (55.12) | (57.88) | (60.19) |
| **(−) $\Delta$NWC (12%)** | (9.60) | (9.07) | (8.32) | (7.35) | (6.17) |
| **Unlevered FCF** | **90.30** | **102.15** | **114.17** | **123.68** | **132.61** |

---

## 5. Step 4: Discount Rate (WACC Calculation)

To discount unlevered cash flows, we compute the Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC):

### Cost of Equity ($K_e$) via CAPM
- Risk-free rate ($R_f$): $4.1\%$
- Equity Risk Premium ($\text{ERP}$): $5.5\%$
- Levered Beta ($\beta$): $1.18$

$$K_e = R_f + \beta \times \text{ERP} = 4.1\% + 1.18 \times 5.5\% = \mathbf{10.5900\%}$$

### After-Tax Cost of Debt ($K_{d,\text{after-tax}}$)
- Pre-tax cost of debt ($K_d$): $6.2\%$
- Marginal tax rate ($t$): $25.0\%$

$$K_{d,\text{after-tax}} = K_d \times (1 - t) = 6.2\% \times (1 - 0.25) = \mathbf{4.6500\%}$$

### Blended WACC
- Equity Weight ($W_e$): $78.0\%$
- Debt Weight ($W_d$): $22.0\%$

$$\text{WACC} = W_e K_e + W_d K_{d,\text{after-tax}} = (0.78 \times 10.5900\%) + (0.22 \times 4.6500\%) = \mathbf{9.2832\%}$$

---

## 6. Step 5: Terminal Value & Present Value

Cash flows beyond 2030E are captured using the **Gordon Growth Perpetuity Formula** with a terminal growth rate $g = 2.5\%$:

### Terminal Year Cash Flow ($FCF_{T+1}$)
$$FCF_{T+1} = \text{UFCF}_{2030E} \times (1 + g) = \$132.6055 \times (1 + 0.025) = \$135.9206\text{mm}$$

### Terminal Value at Horizon ($T = 5$)
$$\text{TV}_{\text{horizon}} = \frac{FCF_{T+1}}{\text{WACC} - g} = \frac{\$135.9206}{0.092832 - 0.025} = \mathbf{\$2,003.7826\text{mm}}$$

### Present Value of Terminal Value
> ⚠️ **Common AI Error**: Adding $\$2,003.78\text{mm}$ directly into Enterprise Value without discounting!

Using end-of-year discounting at $T = 5$:
$$\text{Discount Factor}_{Y5} = \frac{1}{(1 + 0.092832)^5} = 0.641554$$
$$\text{PV}(\text{TV}) = \$2,003.7826 \times 0.641554 = \mathbf{\$1,285.5341\text{mm}}$$

### Present Value of Explicit Forecast (2026E–2030E)
Discounting each year's UFCF yields:
$$\sum \text{PV}(\text{UFCF}) = \$82.63 + \$85.54 + \$87.49 + \$86.72 + \$85.06 = \mathbf{\$427.4318\text{mm}}$$

---

## 7. Step 6: Enterprise Value to Equity Bridge

Now we bridge Enterprise Value (firm value) to Equity Value (shareholder value):

```text
  Enterprise Value:       $1,712.9659mm   (PV of UFCFs + PV of TV)
Less: Total Gross Debt:  ($420.0000mm)
Plus: Cash Balance:       +$95.0000mm
──────────────────────────────────────
= Less: Net Debt:        ($325.0000mm)
──────────────────────────────────────
= Implied Equity Value:   $1,387.9659mm
÷ Diluted Shares:            60.0000mm
──────────────────────────────────────
= Implied Share Price:         $23.1328
```

> ⚠️ **Cash Reversal Bug**: If an analyst accidentally computes $\text{Net Debt} = \text{Debt} + \text{Cash} = \$515\text{mm}$, equity value drops to $\$1,197.97\text{mm}$ (\$19.97/share) — a catastrophic \$190mm valuation error!

## 8. Trading Comps Analysis

In addition to DCF, bankers evaluate how comparable publicly traded peers are valued by the market.

### Peer Multiples Table

| Peer Company | NTM EV/EBITDA | Note | Treatment in Benchmark |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Apex Motion** | 7.9x | Normal comp | Included in median |
| **Beacon Industrial** | 8.5x | Normal comp | Included in median |
| **Crestline Systems** | 9.2x | Sep 30 FY mismatch | Included in median (with disclosure) |
| **Delta Precision** | 7.3x | Lower-margin business | Included in median |
| **Evergreen Controls** | **N/M** | Negative EBITDA | **Excluded from median** (never 0.0x!) |

### Comps Valuation Math
1. **Valid Peer Median**: Across `[7.3, 7.9, 8.5, 9.2]`, the median of the 4 valid peers is:
   $$\text{Median NTM Multiple} = \frac{7.9 + 8.5}{2} = \mathbf{8.20x}$$
2. **Implied Enterprise Value**: Applied to 2026E EBITDA (\$183.60mm):
   $$\text{Comps EV} = 8.20\text{x} \times \$183.60\text{mm} = \mathbf{\$1,505.52\text{mm}}$$
3. **Comps Equity Value**:
   $$\text{Comps Equity} = \$1,505.52\text{mm} - \$325.00\text{mm} = \mathbf{\$1,180.52\text{mm}}$$
4. **Implied Comps Share Price**:
   $$\text{Comps Share Price} = \frac{\$1,180.52\text{mm}}{60.0\text{mm}} = \mathbf{\$19.6753}$$

## 9. The Valuation Tension: DCF vs. Comps

Notice the valuation output:
- **DCF Implied Share Price**: **\$23.13**
- **Comps Implied Share Price**: **\$19.68**
- **Current Trading Price**: **\$20.00**

### What does this teach a senior analyst?
A junior analyst or naive AI might simply take the mathematical average:
$$\frac{\$23.13 + \$19.68}{2} = \$21.41$$

A seasoned banker investigates the **underlying assumptions**:
1. **DCF ($23.13)** reflects management's internal forecast of continuous margin expansion (17.0% → 18.5%) and strong productivity gains.
2. **Comps ($19.68)** reflects current public market pricing, which trades at an 8.2x median multiple and may be discounting raw-material inflation and execution risks.
3. Both methods are internally consistent, but they tell distinct economic stories.
